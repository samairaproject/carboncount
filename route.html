Route Planner

Enter a starting point and a destination and CarbonCount compares driving, cycling, walking, and an estimated transit option side by side, then flags whichever is realistically the lowest-carbon choice for that specific trip. All routes load at the same time, so this is usually a few seconds, not a long wait.

This uses free public OpenStreetMap routing services, so results depend on OSM's map data and the service being online — it's a demo-quality routing feature, not a production-grade GPS app.

🧮 How we calculate these CO₂ numbers

For each mode, we multiply the route's distance by a fixed emissions factor:

Estimated CO₂ (lbs) = Distance (miles) × Emissions factor (lbs CO₂ per passenger-mile)

The factors we use are real, published national averages — not made up placeholders:

  • Driving: 0.89 lbs CO₂/mile — the EPA's figure for a typical U.S. passenger vehicle (404 g CO₂/mile), based on ~22.2 mpg average fuel economy and 8,887g of CO₂ per gallon of gasoline burned. Source: US EPA
  • Public transit: 0.45 lbs CO₂/passenger-mile — the Federal Transit Administration's national average across bus and rail transit, versus 0.96 lbs/mile for a single-occupancy car. It's a blended national figure since we don't have real-time data on your specific bus or train line. Source: FTA, via APTA
  • Walking & cycling: 0 lbs CO₂/mile — no tailpipe emissions. This covers direct emissions only, not the lifecycle footprint of manufacturing a bike or the food calories burned to power the trip.

Distances come from real routed directions (not straight-line "as the crow flies" distance) via OpenStreetMap's OSRM routing engine. Two honest caveats: these are national averages, so your actual car or transit line could be cleaner or dirtier than the average; and these are tailpipe/tank-to-wheel numbers, not full lifecycle emissions (manufacturing, grid mix for electric transit, etc.).

Want to calculate this yourself for your own trip? Take your route's mileage and multiply it by the factor for your mode above — that's the exact math this page runs.
